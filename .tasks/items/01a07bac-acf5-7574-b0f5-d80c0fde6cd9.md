# Declared-device authority and the pwm-servo protocol

## Context

The IO substrate resolves a driver's MMIO authority only by probing the virtio transport window,
so a physical device at a fixed address cannot be granted to a userspace driver at all. This
adds the missing half -- a device the generation *declares* rather than one the root discovers --
together with the first device-specific protocol that uses it, a servo-PWM contract.

The mechanism and the protocol are board-neutral: the driver refuses to program anything unless
its granted page is the device it expects, so the same composition boots under QEMU with the
driver resident and reporting no device.

## Exit conditions

Under QEMU: the `sel4-pwm` composition admits, its driver binds no device and stays resident
answering the protocol's no-device status, graph health stays green, and the boot layout matches
its frozen fixture. On the host: the protocol's Rust and C encoders agree on one pinned request
vector, and the declared-device inventory is covered by a root unit test.
