# Shared-buffer operations permanently consume backing and root CSlots

## Problem

The simultaneous private-memory qualification exposed permanent shared-buffer
resource consumption under an unchanged one-page/one-buffer budget. The existing
global allocator is monotonic; logical buffer release does not restore usable
physical backing or all root CSlots. This prevents the no-drift exit condition of
work item `01a07a2d-00a1-727c-a548-130a4475f5bb` from being met.

## Observed evidence

Direct observations from uncommitted qualification changes on 2026-09-17, not a
claim about a released generation: four 256 MiB private holders completed twenty
fault/restart cycles on both QEMU architectures. Each cycle also performed two
shared-buffer sequences: create one page, refuse a second create, map outside the
private window, seal/read, unmap, and release.

Reusable backing, retained anchors, private mappings and descriptor availability

## Root cause and ownership

- `slime-root/src/graph_runtime/services/capability.rs`, `serve_buffer_create`:
  current quota occupancy is checked by `buffers.create` only after physical
  frames have been allocated. The rejected second create consumes backing.
- `slime-root/src/buffer_adapter.rs`, `ensure_tables`: fresh short-lived adapters
  allocate each intermediate level again on mapping and seal remapping. Both
  successful tables and unused `DeleteFirst` objects lack a durable reusable
  lifetime owner here.
- `BufferAdapter`'s `ReleaseFrame` deletes the kernel capability without returning
  the allocator slot or restoring physical backing/provenance/accounting.
- `slime-root/src/object_allocator.rs`: the global allocation path advances
  untyped watermarks monotonically. Returning a CSlot alone cannot reclaim those
  physical holes. Its live-object counters also retain deleted objects.

A read-only review independently reconciled the RV64 delta: per shared sequence,
one admitted frame, one refused-create frame, two initial-map tables and two
seal-remap tables consume six granules. ARM has three intermediate levels.

## Scope

1. Preflight complete shared-buffer quota/count/total admission before spending
   backing, retaining transactional rollback for subsequent failures.
2. Give shared frames bounded reusable backing with required contiguity and DMA
   provenance preserved. Zero bytes before reassignment to another buffer.
3. Own mapping tables durably by VSpace, level and aligned address; avoid
   speculative retained tables on remap, and reclaim or reuse them when their
   actual lifetime ends. Do not grow a cache indefinitely over retired VSpaces.
4. Preserve outstanding loans, aliases and retryable teardown. Owner release
   while loans remain outstanding must not prematurely reuse backing; creator
   death must follow the existing holder-reclamation semantics.
5. Report retained pools/anchors and reusable bytes honestly. Do not subtract
   leaked capacity from acceptance, relabel it reusable without proving reuse,
   or change the shared-buffer budget to hide consumption.

Prefer a bounded shared-buffer-specific reclamation path over redesigning every
root-global allocation. The exact mechanism must follow existing ownership and
contiguity requirements, not assume task arenas are directly interchangeable.

## Exit conditions

- Repeat successful create/map/seal/unmap/release and rejected second creates
  under an unchanged one-page/one-buffer declaration on both QEMU architectures.
  Equivalent phases show no loss of usable backing, free root CSlots after
  explicitly accounting for bounded retained anchors/pools, or descriptors,
  including after twenty private-holder fault/restart cycles.
- Compare an honest pre-workload baseline with full teardown. Explicitly account
  for bounded retained anchors/pools and prove immediate reuse; all mappings,
  logical quota, physical provenance and non-retained objects are released.
- Different buffers receive zeroed backing. Outstanding loans retain data and
  authority after owner release until final settlement; creator death follows
  existing holder-reclamation semantics without premature reuse or retained
  resource leaks. Failed revoke/unmap/retype retries cannot duplicate ownership,
  double-charge, or free a still-live object.
- Mapping-table reuse distinguishes different VSpaces and addresses. Repeated
  VSpace construction/retirement, seal/remap and already-present table cases do
  not accumulate objects.
- Existing shared-buffer, loan, IO/DMA and private-memory regression gates remain
  green. Extend owning tests and mutation controls rather than add a standalone
  checker for this bug.
