# Global untyped alignment gaps strand reclaimable capacity

## Problem

`slime-root/src/object_allocator.rs::allocate_from_global` aligns the current
ordinary-untyped watermark before retyping an object. A successful allocation
advances past the skipped prefix without retaining it as independently owned,
reusable backing. Reclaiming a task or shared-buffer child extent cannot recover
that prefix; revoking the global parent while unrelated descendants remain live
would be unsafe.

This prevents the cold-baseline conservation required by
`01a07a2d-00a1-727c-a548-130a4475f5bb` and exposed while repairing
`01a0ad2e-965d-7b9c-962b-45f7ab67001f`. Stable repeated-cycle counters do not
prove conservation from the first allocation.

## Direct measurement (2026-09-17)

An uncommitted diagnostic build added successful global-allocation records
(parent, destination slot, prior watermark, aligned start, end, object bytes,
skipped bytes) and active task-extent backing at the initial/final census.

## Scope and invariants

- Preserve skipped ordinary backing before a larger aligned retype consumes it,
  using legal aligned power-of-two child untypeds or an equivalent bounded
  backing allocator that splits parents before issuing extents.
- Track capability, descriptor, physical range and parent/child ownership
  explicitly. Preflight bookkeeping and CSlots before kernel effects; partial
  preservation/split/coalesce failures retain retryable ownership.
- Make preserved backing actually allocatable by subsequent provisioning. A
  ledger entry without a usable capability is not reusable capacity.
- Cover all affected ordinary-global provisioning paths, including task/static,
  private/shared extent parents and contiguous allocations. Do not consume
  device untypeds, invent physical adjacency, or revoke a parent with unrelated
  live descendants. Preserve contiguous DMA provenance and zero-before-reuse.
- Keep requested private/shared payload ceilings unchanged. Report reserved
  backing, alignment, metadata, retained anchors, committed payload and reusable
  capacity separately. No swap, overcommit, larger launcher RAM, or physical
  hardware qualification is implied.
- Prefer a change at the global-parent provisioning boundary over redesigning
  unrelated root mechanisms. Allocation order alone is not a general repair.

## Exit conditions

- On ARM 2 GiB and RV64 3 GiB, reconcile every successful ordinary allocation's
  range with the initial inventory and retained/reusable ownership. Cold baseline
  through full teardown conserves usable capacity, including initial live
  backing and explicitly retained anchors, without unexplained losses.
- Demonstrate immediate allocation from previously skipped prefixes, not merely
  a byte counter. Exercise mixed sizes, repeated fresh/reused extents, fragmented
  parents, and a small allocation followed by a highly aligned one.
- Inject descriptor/CSlot exhaustion, retype/revoke failure and partial-progress
  failure. Retry cannot duplicate, overlap, lose, or prematurely reuse backing;
  existing live data and authority survive. Freed frame bytes are zero on reuse.
- Run the unchanged four-holder residency/restart workload and independent
  shared-buffer operations on both architectures, retaining the original
  workload and no-drift requirements. Do not shift baseline after warm-up.
- Existing root, private-memory, shared-buffer/loan, reclamation and IO/DMA
  regressions pass. Small target metadata/boot constraints remain explicit.
