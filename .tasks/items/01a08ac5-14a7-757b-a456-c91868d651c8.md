# H1V1 UART7 bench probe and pad qualification

## Context

Every fact this lane rests on -- which pad UART7's transmit function reaches, whether that pad
is on a connector, what the clock, reset, and pinmux words hold at the vendor prompt, and
whether a 16550 there can carry 57600 baud into a telemetry radio -- is read from the vendor BSP
and unobserved. This milestone answers them from U-Boot, before any Slime code exists.

The board's own bench probe already drives one pad and restores it; this extends the same
checker with a UART mode, and adds the MAVLink framing and the ground-radio decoder that later
sessions reuse as their instrument.

## Exit conditions

On the named Novatek NT98690 H1V1, from its unmodified vendor U-Boot: UART7's clock, reset,
divider, pinmux, and pad words read their surveyed values before anything is written; the port
takes the divisor and line settings and reads them back; N pinned MAVLink v2 HEARTBEAT frames
written to its transmit register are each decoded on the host's paired radio within a bounded
window, with a valid checksum and consecutive sequence numbers; every shared word the probe
changed is written back to its surveyed value *and read back*; and the vendor firmware banner
returns after `reset`. The pad, its header pin, and the surveyed words are pinned in
`sel4/pins.toml`.

The readback requirement is not incidental. `P6.PWM.A` was closed on a run whose transcript
carried its restoration writes but no readbacks, and was reopened for exactly that gap; this
probe verifies each restoration independently from its first run.

## Verification

Gates: `just nt98690_bench_probe_check`, `just sel4_pin_check`, `just sel4_gate_control_check`

Bench modes assert no marker contract and have no `just` recipe, so the gate count is unchanged.
The board evidence is the run's own transcript and the ground radio's raw capture.

## Exit condition (observed)

2026-09-10, on the named board, from its unmodified vendor U-Boot, with the ground radio on the
development host: the surveyed words read `0x09090909`, `0x00007060`, `0xffffffb7`,
`0x00000001`, `0x00000000`, `0xffffffff` (divider, gate, reset, TOP13, TOP14, pad function);
the port took divisor 52 and 8N1 and read them back; ten heartbeats, sequence 0 through 9, were
each decoded on the ground radio within its window, and the 210-byte capture is byte-identical
to the encoder's output; the three shared words the probe had changed were written back and
read back, the two it had not needed to change were verified; the vendor banner returned after
`reset`. The radio's own parameters were read and match the plan.

The pad's position rests on the wiring of that run -- signal on header pin 13, ground on 14, an
operator report -- and on the frames having arrived through it. The meter reading the bench
steps asked for was not taken; the `--gpio-probe 8` transcript shows the pad toggling and
reading back forty times at the register level, and nothing about the connector. Recorded here
so a later mystery about that pin has one place to start.
